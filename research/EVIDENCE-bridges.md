# EVIDENCE — bridges and structural inspection

An appendix to `research/EVIDENCE.md`, added by the video pass on 16 September
2026 because the main file does not cover this ground. Its own §11 says so:

> **Utilities and infrastructure** (domain 9) is covered only through the PG&E
> case in §3.1, which is strong enough to carry a product on its own. Bridge and
> rail inspection were not researched.

That left Hairline, the crack-width product, with no verified number it was
allowed to put on a slide. FINDINGS.md §5.2 carries an ASCE passage with six
figures, but unlike every other fetched source in that document it names no
saved artefact, only a bare URL, so under the project's own rule it had to be
treated as unverified until checked.

It has now been checked. The page was fetched on **16 September 2026** and the
text is saved at `research/sources/asce-2025-bridges.txt`. **All six figures are
correct, verbatim.** Everything below is quoted from that page.

Source for this whole file: American Society of Civil Engineers, *2025
Infrastructure Report Card*, bridges category page,
<https://infrastructurereportcard.org/cat-item/bridges/>, fetched 16 September
2026, HTTP 200.

## The money

> "ASCE's Bridging the Gap report indicates there is a funding gap of $373
> billion over 10 years to bring the nation's bridges into a state of good
> repair."

> "To bring the nation's bridges into a state of good repair over the next 10
> years, an additional $373 billion in funding is needed."

> "System rehabilitation needs for bridges are estimated at $191.3 billion. As of
> 2023, FHWA estimates the cost to replace poor bridges totals $69.7 billion,
> while rehabilitation costs are $47.4 billion."

## The scale

> "There are 623,218 bridges in the country, with an average age of about 47
> years, that serve as vital connections throughout the transportation system."

> "About a third of the nation's bridge inventory (221,791 spans) needs repair
> work or replacement. Approximately 45% of bridges have exceeded their planned
> design lives of 50 years."

> "Of these bridges, 49.1% are in fair condition, 44.1% are in good condition,
> and 6.8% are in poor condition — a number that has continued to decline over
> the past few years."

> "63,085 of the nation's 623,218 bridges were posted for load in 2024, meaning
> they have restrictions regarding the weight of vehicles that can safely cross."

> "In 2024, over 4.9 billion motor vehicle trips were taken daily across the
> nation's 623,218 bridges."

## The failure mode, and the named investigation

This is the bridges equivalent of the HSE camera line in §5.3: a regulator saying
that the inspection itself failed.

> "Maintenance lessons can be learned from the collapse of Pittsburgh's Fern
> Hollow Bridge, which took place in January 2022 after experiencing a structural
> failure. The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) concluded that
> critical lapses in maintenance and oversight by multiple agencies led to the
> bridge's collapse. Although previous inspections had repeatedly documented
> issues with the bridge, maintenance and repairs were not performed to resolve
> these issues. Additionally, Pennsylvania Department of Transportation (PennDOT)
> contractors conducted inspections that did not comply with guidance and failed
> to identify fracture-critical areas on the bridge's legs."

Two separate failures in one paragraph, and a measurement tool only addresses the
second: inspections that were carried out but did not record what was there. Say
that plainly rather than implying a camera would have prevented the collapse.
The first failure was that documented problems were not acted on, and no
measurement product fixes that.

## What this page does not say

- It gives no crack-width threshold, no inspection interval and no figure for how
  often a crack is mismeasured. Nothing here supports a claim about the accuracy
  of current practice.
- The ASCE letter grade for bridges does not appear in the fetched text, so do
  not quote one.
- It says remote sensors, ultrasound and drones "can help engineers identify
  problems earlier". It does not evaluate any of them, and it says nothing about
  phone photography or marker-based scale.
